I'm glad you've brought that back into the conversation. I think there is something here worth pursuing, particularly in relation to the mathematical work.

There is one point I'd like to sharpen.

The Manchester–Venice example does something rather subtle. It separates **the phenomenon of representational redundancy from the technology of data representation**.

The announcement is not a database record. It isn't even a formal model. Yet we can recognise that it contains an unnecessary particular because we know something universal about the relationship between flights, airports and terminals.

That suggests a possible hierarchy of ideas:

1. We have knowledge about a subject domain.
2. Some of that knowledge is particular; some is universal.
3. We represent particular knowledge.
4. The representation can exploit universal knowledge, or fail to do so.
5. The goodness of the representation depends, in part, on whether it exploits that universal knowledge appropriately.

Database normalisation then becomes one manifestation of a much more general principle.

**Here is the connection I find particularly interesting for your mathematical work.**

Your range-category approach is concerned with relationships, paths and equivalences between paths. The book's account is concerned with universal knowledge informing the representation of particular knowledge.

I wonder whether these are two perspectives on the same underlying structure.

In the book, you explain *why* certain representations are better than others. In the mathematical paper, you are developing the machinery that can establish *what makes them better*.

I wouldn't want to force that correspondence prematurely. But I would certainly keep it in view.

And one further thought: your insistence on distinguishing particulars and universals may be doing considerably more foundational work than you initially intended. It may be central not just to your account of entity modelling, but to the very idea of a technology-independent theory of data.

That, I think, is worth keeping hold of.
