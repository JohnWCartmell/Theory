\begin{aside}
13th October 2026. I have gravitated toward starting with 
poset-enriched bisupport catgories rather than range categories. 
This is  because the proofs regarding tightness needed to establish 
a category of tight zigzags benefit 
from appeals to symmetry of support and cosupport. 

\begin{equation}
\label{posetEnrichedBisupportCategory}
\text{poset enriched bisupport category}
\end{equation}
There is a category \cat{pobicat}.

The category of zigzags over a poset enriched bisupport category
is also a poset enriched bisupport category. Additionally it has a converse operation. 
The converse operation  satisfies two of three conditions necessary for it to be
said to be compatible with the bisupport structure.
To satisfy the third condition, a quotient category of the category of zigzags has to be taken but this quotient can also be described more directly 
as the category of tight zigzags over the underlying bisupport category.

A further compatibility condition is appropriate if some or all morphisms in the underlying bisupport category are elected to be representative of functional
relationships also said to be detrministic. The extreme case is that of a range category where all morphisms are deemed thus. For such morphisms $f$ the converse operation should meet the additional compatibility axiom
\[
\converse{f} \circ f = \rng{f}.
\]

Similarly a morphisim may be deemed to represent a real world relationship whose \textit{converse} is functional. For such morphisms the appropriate compatibility axiom will be
\[
f \circ \converse{f} = \rst{f}
\]

One aspect of traditional software analysis and design methodologies 
is a sharp focus on data requirements 
and a rigourous description of 
those aspects of real-world entities and binary relationships 
such as are to be represented 
within the data of the software system. 
For many such methods these descriptions are written as 
annotated graphs in which nodes are types of entity and edges are binary relationships. Annotations include cardinality constraints, for instance a relationship $r$ that is defined to be between entitites of type $a$ and entitites of type $b$ is said to be cardinality zero-or-one if for any entity $a$ of type $A$ there is at most one entity $b$ such that a R b.
Such a relationship can also said to be \textit{functional} or \textit{function-like} or \textit{deterministic}. 
Now to any binary relationship $r: a \morph b$ there is a converse relationship by which entities of type $b$ are related to entities of type $b$. 
In this paper this converse 
will be written as $\converse{r} : b \morph a$. 
In software design notation the the whole will likely be represented as a single edge annotated and possibly named at both ends. 


\begin{equation}
\label{functionAwarePosetEnrichedBisupportWithConverse}
\text{function aware poset enriched bisupport category with compatible converse}
\end{equation}
There is a category \cat{fawpobicatcon}. 

The category of short tight zigzags in addition satisfies condition K.4.
This is currently described in the section on bisupport categories with converse. It states that distinguised function like morphisms satisfy
$\converse{f} \circ f = \rng{f}$.
